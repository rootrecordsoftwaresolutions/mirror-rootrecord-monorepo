# The Individual Switch

## Deleting the Map

---

### Foreword: A Word From The Fence

I am not a politician. I am not a pundit. I am not a professor with a tweed jacket and a chalkboard.

I am a man who grew up in Middle Tennessee, lived in seven states, and finally got tired enough of the noise to sail off the edge of the map. I now live on the Big Island of Hawaii. I make my own power. I catch my own water. I cook my own food.

And from way out here, I can see the mainland real clear.

What I see is a country full of decent people held hostage by two rival gangs, drowning in two diseases I call the Double Ds: **Debt and Doubt**.

This little book is not a manifesto. It is not a plan to "change the world." Changing the world is a fool's errand and a full-time job for liars. This is something simpler.

This book is a switch.

It is already wired. It is already installed. It has been sitting on the wall your whole life, waiting on a finger.

I'm just here to point at it.

## Chapter 1: The Misrepresented Middle

There is a quiet group in this country that nobody talks about. The news won't cover them. The parties won't fund them. The pollsters can't sort them.

I call them the **Misrepresented Middle**.

They are not "moderates." Moderate is a weak word. It sounds like lukewarm coffee. These folks are not lukewarm. They are independent thinkers who have looked at both teams and decided neither one is wearing a clean jersey.

They believe in hard work and a fair shake. They believe a man should keep what he earns and help his neighbor when the neighbor is down. They believe in the door being open and the porch light being on, but they also believe in a sturdy lock.

They are not red. They are not blue. They are tired.

### The Rival Gangs

The two-party system is not a debate. It's a turf war.

Both gangs need each other. They are not opposites. They are dance partners. One side spits at the other on television, and then they go to the same dinner together. They wear different colors so you'll pick a side, because as long as you are fighting your neighbor, you aren't looking at them.

The Rival Gangs do not survive on ideas. They survive on division. Division is the only product they sell, and the American people are buying it by the truckload.

That is the trick. That is the whole magic show.

You do not fix a rigged game by playing it harder. You stand up from the table.

### Common Sense Check

If two organizations need you to hate each other in order to stay in business, those two organizations are not on your side. They are on each other's side. You are the lunch.

## Chapter 2: Individual Votership

We have been told for a long time that voting is a sacred right. I agree with that. But somewhere along the way, the right got chopped up and handed to teams instead of people.

Right now, you don't really vote. You endorse a gang. You pull a lever and a thousand strangers ride in on it, half of whom you would not let pet your dog.

That has to end.

I'm not talking about reform. Reform is what they sell you when they want to keep the furniture and just rearrange it. I'm talking about **abolition** of the two-party system as we know it, and replacing it with something honest.

I call it **Individual Votership**.

### What That Means

You do not vote for a party.

You do not vote for a "platform" written by people who have never met you.

You vote for a person, on a single issue, at a single time, and that vote is yours. It does not get bundled. It does not get traded. It does not get sold to a lobbyist in a back room six months later.

If a candidate says they will fix the road, the road is the contract. If the road is not fixed, the contract is broken, and they are gone. Not next election. Now.

It is membership, not marriage. You are not stuck with a politician until death. You are a paying customer, and the customer can leave.

### Why The Gangs Hate This

The gangs hate Individual Votership because it kills the bundle. Right now they sell you a value meal. You wanted the burger, but you also got fries you didn't ask for and a soda that gives you cancer. Individual Votership lets you order off the menu.

Once you can order off the menu, the kitchen has to actually cook.

### Common Sense Check

A vote should be a sentence, not a paragraph. If you can't explain what your vote did in one breath, somebody else is breathing for you.

## Chapter 3: Behavioral Maps vs. County Lines

Look at a map of the United States. You will see a bunch of straight lines and squiggly lines drawn by men who have been dead two hundred years. Those lines tell you who you are, who you can vote for, and what laws you live under.

That is insane.

A line on a piece of paper does not know your name. It does not know if your town is taking care of you or robbing you blind. It does not care if your sheriff is a good man or a punk with a badge.

So delete the map.

### The Behavioral Map

Replace county lines with **behavioral maps**.

A behavioral map is simple. You belong to a town because you choose to belong to it. You spend your money there. You sleep there. You raise your kids there. You shake hands with the mayor at the gas station.

If you associate with a town, you **register** with it. Your taxes go there. Your vote goes there. Your loyalty goes there.

But here is the kicker.

If that town fails you — if the schools rot, if the cops stop showing up, if the council steals from the till — you do not have to wait four years for a referendum. You move your **membership** instantly.

Pick up the phone. Click a button. You are now a registered citizen of the next town over. Your tax dollars follow you. Your vote follows you.

### Voting With Your Feet, And Your Commerce

The mainland already has this in raw form. People leave dying states for living ones every day. They are voting with their feet. The problem is the system punishes them for it. Paperwork. Residency requirements. Fees. Waiting periods designed to keep you trapped while the rats finish the cheese.

Individual Votership and behavioral maps end that.

A town that does its job keeps its members. A town that doesn't, doesn't.

This is not socialism. This is not capitalism. This is **Yelp** for government, and frankly it is overdue.

### The Shit Show

Some cities right now are what I call **The Shit Show**. You know the ones. Tents on every corner. Needles in the parks. Roads that look like the moon. Mayors who blame everyone but the mirror.

Under behavioral mapping, the Shit Show ends fast. Because the customer leaves. The dollars leave. The Shit Show either cleans itself up, or it dies. Either outcome is an upgrade.

A government that cannot lose its customers will never serve them.

### Common Sense Check

You should be able to fire your town as easy as you fire your cell phone carrier. Until you can, the town is not working for you. You are working for it.

## Chapter 4: Fix Your Own Front Door

There is a pothole on every road in America. You know the one. You have hit it twice this week. You have cussed at it. You have called somebody about it.

Nothing happened.

That pothole is not a road problem. It is a philosophy problem.

### The Ownership Model

The mainland runs on a strange idea: that everything outside your front door belongs to "them." Some agency. Some department. Some manager you've never met in a building you've never been to.

So nobody fixes anything. Because nobody owns anything.

Out here on the Big Island, if a pig roots up my driveway, I fix the driveway. I do not file a request with the Department of Driveways. There is no Department of Driveways. There is me, a shovel, a wheelbarrow, and a couple of hours.

The mainland needs to remember that feeling.

If you own the property, you own the front door. The road in front of your house is part of your front door. The sidewalk is part of your front door. The drainage ditch is part of your front door.

Government's job is not to do it for you. Government's job is to **not get in your way** when you do it yourself, and to chip in for the parts that cross many doors at once.

### First Responders Are Utilities

This one is going to make some folks mad, and I'm fine with that.

Police, fire, and medical are utilities. Same as water. Same as power. Same as internet.

You pay for performance. You fire for failure.

Right now first responders are bundled into the same lump of taxes that pays for the mayor's lunch and the council's pet projects. There is no clean line between what you paid in and what service you got back.

That has to end.

If my fire department shows up in four minutes, that is a five-star utility, and I will gladly pay top dollar. If it shows up in forty minutes after the house is already a slab, that utility is fired. I should be able to switch providers like I switch internet.

Same with police. A department that protects and serves keeps its contract. A department that hides in the parking lot and writes speeding tickets to fund itself loses its contract.

This is not "defunding." This is **accounting**.

If first responders are tied to performance, the good ones get rich and the bad ones get gone. Right now we do the opposite. We protect the bad ones with unions and pensions and call the good ones "lucky."

### Common Sense Check

You wouldn't pay your power bill if the lights never came on. Stop paying for government services that never come on.

## Chapter 5: The Hard Reset

Let's talk about money.

The dollar in your pocket is not real. I am not being poetic. I am being literal. It is a number on a screen at a bank, backed by the promise of a government that has openly admitted, under oath, that it has lost track of **trillions**.

Trillions. Not millions. Not billions. **Trillions.**

If your wife came home and said she lost track of a hundred bucks, you would have a conversation. If she said she lost track of a hundred thousand, you would have an argument. If she said she lost track of a few trillion, you would change the locks.

We have not changed the locks. We have given them a bigger house.

### Debt and Doubt

The Double Ds are simple.

**Debt** is the number on the books. It is the official IOU. It is so big now that nobody alive will ever pay it back. We all know that. The politicians know that. The bankers know that. The only people pretending otherwise are the ones still selling bonds.

**Doubt** is the feeling you get when you watch the news, look at your bank account, and then look at the price of eggs. Doubt is what makes you wonder if working hard is even worth it anymore. Doubt is the disease the dollar gave you.

You cannot reform a currency built on Debt and Doubt. You have to reset it.

### The Hard Reset

I am proposing a **Hard Reset**.

A new currency. **Digital-first**, but **physical-backed**. Debt-free from day one. Un-fudgeable.

Digital-first means it lives on a ledger. Every dollar is tracked. Every dollar has a serial. Every transaction is on the books. There is no "missing trillion," because the math is public, all the way down.

Physical-backed means every digital dollar represents something you can put your hands on. Gold. Silver. Land. Energy. Pick the basket. The point is, the screen number ties back to a real-world thing, and that thing has weight.

Debt-free means the currency is not loaned into existence. It is not created by a private bank charging interest to the country for the privilege of using its own money. That is the scam we have been running since 1913, and it is the engine of the Double Ds.

Un-fudgeable means no surprise printing. No quiet inflation to bail out a bank. No "we found seventy billion in the couch cushions." If a new dollar gets made, it gets made because something real backs it, and the public sees it happen.

### Why This Terrifies The Right People

You will know this idea is correct because the loudest, richest, most-connected people will scream the loudest about it. Good. Let them scream.

A debt-free, transparent, physical-backed currency cuts the legs off three industries at once: the central bank, the lobbyist class, and the political donor class. All three live off the fog. The Hard Reset turns on the lights.

### Common Sense Check

If you can't count it, you don't own it. Right now, nobody is counting. So nobody owns anything. Including you.

## Chapter 6: The Social Dividend

Let me tell you about a magic trick the government plays.

It is called the **General Fund**.

Here is how it works. You pay a tax for a thing. Let's say you pay a gas tax to fix the roads. Good. Roads need fixing. Fair deal.

The gas tax brings in more than the roads cost. That happens. It is supposed to. So now there is a surplus.

Where does the surplus go?

It goes into the General Fund. And the General Fund goes to whatever the politicians feel like buying that week. Statues. Studies. Foreign wars. A new wing on a building nobody asked for. A consultant who is somebody's brother-in-law.

The surplus you paid, for the road you drive on, gets spent on a piano lesson in another state.

That is theft with extra steps.

### The Social Dividend

Replace the General Fund with the **Social Dividend**.

The rule is simple, and it is non-negotiable.

If a government utility runs a surplus, **the surplus goes back to the people who paid into it.**

The DOT runs a surplus on gas tax? That money gets divided up among the drivers who paid it. Check in the mail. Direct deposit. Whatever.

The water department runs a surplus? Refund.

The trash service runs a surplus? Refund.

There is no "general" fund. There is no "discretionary" fund. There is no slush. Every dollar has a job, and if the job gets done with money left over, that money goes home.

### Why This Matters

Right now, surplus is the enemy of the citizen. A department that runs efficient gets punished. Its budget gets cut next year and the leftover money gets stolen this year.

Under the Social Dividend, efficient departments are loved. The harder they work, the more they save, the bigger the dividend that comes back to the people. Suddenly the citizen and the department are on the same team. Imagine that.

This is not a handout. It is a **refund**. It is your money. You overpaid. You get the change back. Like a normal transaction. Like an honest one.

### Common Sense Check

If a business charged you for ten gallons of gas and only put eight in the tank, you would call the cops. When the government does it, they call it a "fund." Same crime. Different stationery.

## Chapter 7: The Big Island Blueprint

People ask me why I left.

I lived in seven states on the mainland. I saw the good. I saw the bad. I saw the ugly. I saw the in-between, which is the worst of the three because it tricks you into thinking the system is just a little broken instead of all the way broken.

Eventually I did the math. The math said the system was not designed by accident. It was designed on purpose, and the purpose was to keep people **comfortably miserable**. Comfortable enough not to revolt. Miserable enough not to think.

So I voted with my feet.

I moved to the Big Island of Hawaii. And out here, I learned that almost everything I thought was a "political" problem was actually a **lifestyle** problem dressed up in a tie.

### The Blueprint

The Big Island is not paradise. Don't let the postcards lie to you. It is hard country. The lava is real. The weather is real. The cost of a head of lettuce will make you weep.

But it works. It works in a way the mainland forgot how to work.

Here is the blueprint, in plain English:

**1. You make your own power.**
Solar. Wind. Generator. Whatever. The grid is a leash. Cut it, and you stop being a hostage to a utility company that does not love you back.

**2. You catch your own water.**
A roof and a tank. That's it. Rain is free. Always has been.

**3. You grow some of your own food.**
You will not feed your whole family from a backyard garden. Don't pretend. But you can take a real bite out of the grocery bill and a real bite out of your dependence. Both bites taste good.

**4. You know your neighbors.**
Not their politics. Their **names**. Their kids' names. What they drive. What they need. Out here, the guy down the road might be a hippie, a hunter, a Hawaiian elder, a software guy from Seattle, a vet, a farmer, or all six in one. Nobody cares. We just need to know who's got the chainsaw when the tree comes down.

**5. You don't talk down to anybody.**
Diversity on the mainland is a talking point. Out here it is a Tuesday. We have every color, every faith, every background you can imagine, all stacked on a rock in the middle of the ocean. It works because nobody is trying to score points. It works because **respect** is the local currency, and self-sufficiency is the local language.

That is the blueprint. That is the whole thing. You can run it on a rock in the Pacific or a holler in Tennessee. The location doesn't matter. The principles do.

### The Switch Is On The Wall

Everything in this book is already possible. Every chapter. Every idea. Every piece.

Individual Votership? Possible.
Behavioral maps? Possible.
Ownership of your own front door? Possible.
First responders as utilities? Possible.
Hard Reset on the currency? Possible.
Social Dividend? Possible.
Big Island Blueprint? I'm doing it right now.

The switch is on the wall. It always has been.

The only question is whether you are brave enough to flip it.

I'm not here to flip it for you. I tried that. It doesn't work. You cannot drag a man to freedom. He has to walk.

But I can stand on this porch, with a cup of coffee made from beans that grew fifty feet away, and I can point at the wall.

The switch is right there.

Flip it.

### Common Sense Check

You don't need permission to live free. You need a plan, a pair of work boots, and the guts to stop waiting on somebody else to save you. Nobody is coming. That's the bad news. That's also the good news.
