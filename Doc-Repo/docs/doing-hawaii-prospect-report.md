# Doing Hawaii Prospect Report
## Public Website Review, Partnership Fit, and RootRecord Opportunity Plan

Prepared for an in-person discussion with Doing Hawaii.

Scope note: this report is based on publicly accessible pages at `doinghawaii.net`, indexed search results, and visible site navigation as of May 2026. No private subscriber account, analytics, payment dashboard, server logs, or backend access was used.

---

# Executive Summary

Doing Hawaii is a strong partnership prospect for RootRecord because it already has the core ingredients that make a local digital product valuable: a focused audience, original Hawaii Island content, Kilauea relevance, a personal creator story, an existing supporter model, and a clear need for better content packaging.

The public site presents Doing Hawaii as a creator-owned content hub centered around Hawaii experiences, Kilauea, videos, photo albums, wallpapers, games, donations, subscriber access, and online shop ideas. The About page is especially valuable because it explains the origin story: the channel began in October 2013, gained serious momentum during the May 3, 2018 Kilauea eruption, and reportedly grew from 34 subscribers to 6,000+ in a few months, later reaching 13,000+ and growing.

That story is a strong fit for RootRecord's Kilauea Alerts direction. Doing Hawaii has creator credibility around the 2018 eruption and an audience that cares about Kilauea. RootRecord has the app infrastructure, Android distribution, web dashboards, cloud APIs, media/gallery patterns, and Kilauea-specific product surface.

The best opportunity is not a simple ad sale. The stronger opportunity is a content and technology partnership:

- Doing Hawaii contributes original creator content, public videos, photos, wallpapers, eruption history, and audience trust.
- RootRecord provides app placement, media/gallery infrastructure, Android/web product integration, content organization, and custom feature development.
- The combined offer gives Doing Hawaii a better digital home for content while giving Kilauea Alerts richer local media and a stronger creator connection.

Recommended opening position: present RootRecord as a practical technology partner that can help Doing Hawaii modernize, organize, distribute, and monetize content without replacing the creator's identity or audience relationship.

# Brand and Audience Analysis

## What Doing Hawaii Already Has

Doing Hawaii has a compelling creator foundation:

- A personal Hawaii story
- A long-running channel history dating back to 2013
- A Kilauea eruption origin point that changed the channel trajectory
- A subscriber community that grew significantly during the 2018 eruption
- Existing public content and member-content ambition
- Visual media assets such as videos, photos, wallpapers, and channel art
- A donation/supporter model
- A local and visitor-friendly Hawaii Island angle

The strongest differentiator is authenticity. Doing Hawaii is not just a generic travel page. The About story connects the creator directly to the 2018 Kilauea eruption and the experience of documenting events as they unfolded.

## Likely Audience Segments

The public site suggests multiple audience groups:

- Kilauea and volcano watchers
- Hawaii Island residents
- Hawaii visitors and trip planners
- People who followed the 2018 eruption
- YouTube subscribers
- Photo/video viewers
- Supporters who want exclusive content
- People looking for Hawaii wallpapers and visual media
- Local community followers
- People who prefer creator-led Hawaii content over official agency pages

These overlap strongly with Kilauea Alerts users.

## Audience Fit With Kilauea Alerts

Kilauea Alerts is focused on official-source conditions, weather, alerts, earthquakes, live feeds, AI-assisted summaries, and Kilauea context.

Doing Hawaii is focused on creator content, videos, photos, eruption experience, and Hawaii storytelling.

Together, the fit is natural:

- Kilauea Alerts gives users official-source situational context.
- Doing Hawaii can give users local creator perspective, visuals, and community connection.
- RootRecord can keep the safety-critical and official-source content separate from creator content while still giving both a place in the app ecosystem.

This matters because Kilauea content should be handled responsibly. RootRecord can position Doing Hawaii content as "creator media" or "local perspective," not official emergency guidance.

# Website Issues and Improvement Opportunities

This section should be presented carefully in person. The goal is not to criticize. The better framing is: "There is already a lot of value here, and the next step is to organize and modernize it so the audience can access it more easily."

## 1. Mixed Subscription Messaging

The homepage promotes monthly subscribers and "Become Ohana." Gated pages promote subscriber perks. But the Subscription Packages page says:

"I do apologize, website subscription services are no longer available."

This creates confusion. A visitor may not know whether subscriptions are active, inactive, free, paid, paused, or being rebuilt.

Recommended fix:

- Choose one current support model.
- Make the wording consistent on every page.
- If subscriptions are paused, promote email signup or donation instead.
- If subscriptions are coming back, say "Coming soon" and collect interest.

RootRecord opportunity:

- Build a cleaner member/supporter landing page.
- Connect support options to app placements, galleries, or exclusive content.
- Replace unclear gated pages with a modern access flow.

## 2. Under Construction Messaging

The homepage says the site is under construction and that some features will be available soon. Some pages also say perks are subject to change.

This is honest, but it reduces confidence if left too prominent.

Recommended fix:

- Keep "under construction" only where necessary.
- Replace broad warnings with specific roadmap language.
- Show what is available now, what is coming soon, and how to support development.

RootRecord opportunity:

- Turn the under-construction status into a polished roadmap.
- Create a "Doing Hawaii relaunch" campaign.
- Build a phased migration plan.

## 3. Gated Content Without a Clear Current Offer

The video, photo, and games pages show sign-in prompts and subscriber perk examples. But if subscription packages are unavailable, the user may hit a dead end.

Recommended fix:

- Make public previews visible.
- Show sample content before asking for sign-in.
- Give a clear action: donate, join waitlist, subscribe, or follow.

RootRecord opportunity:

- Create a preview gallery.
- Build public/free and supporter/member tiers.
- Add content cards that can also be featured inside Kilauea Alerts.

## 4. Search and Discovery

No sitemap was found. Public search visibility may be limited by sparse page titles, repeated text, gated pages, and under-construction content.

Recommended fix:

- Add sitemap and robots files.
- Add descriptive page titles and meta descriptions.
- Create public landing pages for major content categories.
- Add structured content around Kilauea, 2018 eruption, videos, photos, wallpapers, and supporter options.

RootRecord opportunity:

- Create an SEO-friendly content portal.
- Build landing pages that also support app discovery.

## 5. Mobile and Modern App Opportunity

The site has useful content categories but does not yet feel like a modern mobile-first content app. RootRecord can help turn the content library into a cleaner app-like experience.

Recommended direction:

- Modern responsive web experience
- Better media gallery
- Clear support tiers
- Integration with Kilauea Alerts where appropriate
- Optional custom Doing Hawaii mobile/web app later

# Safety, Trust, and Positioning Notes

Because Doing Hawaii and Kilauea Alerts both touch volcano-related topics, trust and wording matter.

Recommended language:

- "Creator perspective"
- "Local media"
- "Visual storytelling"
- "Kilauea-related content"
- "Follow official agencies for safety decisions"

Avoid language that implies:

- Official authority
- Emergency guidance
- Guaranteed eruption predictions
- Replacement for USGS/HVO/NWS/Hawaiʻi County Civil Defense

For Kilauea Alerts integration, creator content should be clearly separated from official-source alerts and summaries.

Suggested disclaimer:

"Doing Hawaii content is creator media and local perspective. For official volcano, weather, tsunami, and emergency guidance, follow USGS/HVO, National Weather Service, PTWC, Hawaiʻi County Civil Defense, and other emergency authorities."

# Legal and Practical Considerations

This section is not legal advice. It identifies issues worth discussing before any partnership.

## Content Permission

RootRecord should get written permission before displaying Doing Hawaii photos, videos, wallpapers, or branded assets.

Minimum permission points:

- What content can be used
- Where it can appear
- Whether it can appear in app, web, social, or promotional material
- Attribution text
- Whether content can be edited, resized, cropped, or previewed
- Whether content is public or member-only
- Term length and removal process

## Lava Sample / Shop Items

The shop page references 2018 Kilauea eruption lava samples collected from private property and available to US addresses only as donation-related gifts.

This may be legitimate, but because lava/rock collection and sale can be sensitive depending on source, land, permits, shipping, park boundaries, cultural concerns, and platform policies, RootRecord should be careful about promoting physical lava samples without reviewing details.

Recommended approach:

- Do not make the lava sample offer the first partnership focus.
- If it comes up, ask for collection rights, property details, and comfort with public promotion.
- Keep the initial partnership centered on media/content, not physical samples.

## Official Agency Separation

Doing Hawaii content should not be presented as official USGS/HVO/NWS content.

## Sponsorship Fit

Any sponsored content inside RootRecord apps should be relevant, calm, and non-misleading.
