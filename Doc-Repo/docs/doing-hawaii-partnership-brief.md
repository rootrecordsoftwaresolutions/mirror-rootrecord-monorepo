# Doing Hawaii + RootRecord Partnership Opportunity Brief

Prepared for Doing Hawaii  
Prepared by RootRecord  
May 2026

---

# Purpose

This brief outlines a possible partnership between Doing Hawaii and RootRecord. It is intended as a conversation starter, not a final contract or commitment.

Doing Hawaii has a strong creator foundation: original Hawaii Island content, a long-running public presence, Kilauea relevance, an existing audience, and a personal story connected to the 2018 eruption. RootRecord has app infrastructure, Android and web product experience, cloud APIs, media-gallery patterns, user account systems, and Kilauea-focused product work through Kilauea Alerts.

The opportunity is to explore whether Doing Hawaii content and RootRecord technology can support each other in a way that keeps Doing Hawaii's creator identity intact while giving the audience a cleaner, more useful digital experience.

---

# Executive Summary

Doing Hawaii and RootRecord appear to have a natural audience overlap around Hawaii Island, Kilauea, local storytelling, visual media, and people who want timely or meaningful Hawaii-related content.

Doing Hawaii brings creator trust, original media, history, and community interest. RootRecord brings app development, account infrastructure, alerting tools, media presentation, and a growing Kilauea-focused product surface.

A strong partnership would not need to begin as a large project. It could start with a simple, low-risk pilot:

- Feature Doing Hawaii as a local creator/media partner.
- Organize selected public content into a polished mobile-friendly experience.
- Explore Kilauea Alerts placements for creator media, clearly separated from official emergency or safety information.
- Build toward optional supporter features, content galleries, sponsorships, or custom app experiences if the pilot works well.

The key principle: Doing Hawaii remains the creator brand. RootRecord provides technical support, product structure, and distribution options.

# Why Doing Hawaii Stands Out

Doing Hawaii already has several ingredients that are hard to manufacture:

- A clear Hawaii Island focus.
- A personal creator story.
- Public video and visual content.
- A history connected to the 2018 Kilauea eruption.
- A supporter/donation concept already introduced to the audience.
- Existing social links and a content hub direction.
- A recognizable brand presence through channel art, profile imagery, and public pages.

The strongest point is authenticity. Doing Hawaii is not just a generic travel or content page. The public story connects the creator to real Hawaii Island experience and to a moment when many people were looking for eruption-related updates, videos, and perspective.

That kind of creator connection can complement RootRecord's more structured app and alerting work.

# Audience Fit

Doing Hawaii's likely audience includes:

- Hawaii Island residents.
- Hawaii visitors and trip planners.
- Kilauea and volcano watchers.
- People who followed the 2018 eruption.
- YouTube and social media viewers.
- Supporters interested in creator-led local content.
- People looking for Hawaii photos, videos, wallpapers, and community media.

Kilauea Alerts users are likely to include some of the same groups. RootRecord's Kilauea Alerts product focuses on official-source conditions, weather, earthquakes, alerts, live feeds, and AI-assisted summaries.

Doing Hawaii can add a different layer: local creator perspective, visual storytelling, and community connection.

Those two lanes should stay clearly separated:

- RootRecord/Kilauea Alerts: official-source conditions, alerts, weather, earthquakes, and safety-adjacent information.
- Doing Hawaii: creator media, local perspective, photos, videos, history, and community content.

That separation protects both brands and gives users a clearer experience.

# Partnership Ideas

## 1. Creator Media Placement

RootRecord could feature selected Doing Hawaii public content inside a dedicated creator/media area. This could include:

- Featured videos.
- Photo highlights.
- Wallpaper collections.
- Kilauea-related creator content.
- Links back to Doing Hawaii's main channels.

This would give Doing Hawaii another distribution path while giving RootRecord users richer local content.

## 2. Kilauea Alerts Content Companion

Doing Hawaii content could appear as a companion layer near Kilauea Alerts features, clearly labeled as creator media rather than official guidance.

Examples:

- "Local creator perspective" section.
- "Featured Hawaii Island media" cards.
- "From Doing Hawaii" links.
- Historical 2018 eruption media references, if approved and properly framed.

## 3. Modern Content Hub

RootRecord could help shape a cleaner content hub for Doing Hawaii's existing categories:

- Videos.
- Photo albums.
- Wallpapers.
- Supporter content.
- Games or light interactive features.
- Shop or sponsor links.

This could start as a simple landing page or become a larger web/app experience over time.

## 4. Supporter and Sponsorship Path

If Doing Hawaii wants to rebuild or clarify a supporter model, RootRecord could help structure the experience around:

- Free public previews.
- Support/donation calls to action.
- Optional supporter-only content.
- Sponsor placements.
- Email or account-based interest lists.
- Clear "available now" vs. "coming soon" messaging.

No final model needs to be chosen immediately. The first step can be discovery and a small pilot.

## 5. Custom Product Development

If the relationship grows, RootRecord could support custom features such as:

- A Doing Hawaii branded web experience.
- A mobile-friendly media library.
- Creator profile and archive pages.
- Partner landing pages.
- Local business or tourism content tools.
- Sponsor reporting or placement pages.

# Suggested First Pilot

A practical first pilot could be:

## Doing Hawaii Featured Partner Page

RootRecord creates a clean partner page or in-app section that introduces Doing Hawaii and links to approved public content.

Possible content:

- Short Doing Hawaii introduction.
- Creator story summary.
- Selected public videos or playlists.
- Selected photo/media links.
- Links to Doing Hawaii website and social channels.
- Optional support/donation link, if Doing Hawaii wants it included.

## Kilauea Alerts Placement

RootRecord adds a small, clearly labeled creator-media placement in Kilauea Alerts:

"Featured local creator: Doing Hawaii"

This should not interrupt emergency or official-source alert content. It should sit as an optional media/community section.

## Review Period

After launch, both sides review:

- Does the placement feel brand-safe?
- Are users clicking through?
- Is the content presented correctly?
- Does Doing Hawaii want deeper integration?
- Should a sponsor/supporter path be added?

# Important Boundaries

For clarity and trust, any partnership should respect these boundaries:

- Doing Hawaii content should be presented as creator content or local perspective.
- Official alerts, emergency guidance, weather, earthquakes, and government-source information should remain separate.
- RootRecord should not imply that creator content replaces official emergency sources.
- Content ownership, permissions, revenue sharing, sponsor rules, and brand usage should be written down before public launch.
- Any paid supporter or sponsorship model should be reviewed carefully before being promoted.

These boundaries keep the partnership clean and help protect both sides.
